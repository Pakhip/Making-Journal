## Action Plan

Through the progress report critique session I had gained a sense of how my paper garden project is developing. And the most significant feedback that I had received was making the final project into a tactile piece. Several people have also noted that the interactive element where people add their own acts of care is a strong feature because it reinforces the theme of the collective contribution

A suggestion that Leo had given me had encouraged me to think more intentionally about texture because if people are to touch the final outcome. This had connected back directly to the paper that I had selected for the leaves which had a subtle texture to it. This small detail had made the leaves feel more expressive and the feedback had confirmed that this tactile choice matters.

Another important takeaway that came from reviewing other projects was that the data must feel purposeful. This reaffirmed that my qualitative crowdsourced data was the right dataset for the project that is grounded in care and lived experience.


## Project Development

After class and once I was back home I finished drawing the different patterns for the acts of care. As I looked over them I had realised that almost all the patterns naturally were in the forms of circles or rounded shapes. There wasn't really a reason behind why i chose those shapes just that the shapes simply fit the leaf shape more comfortably and created a softer, more organic look.

![all the patterns finished](../assets/week-10/leaf%20patterns%20finished.jpg)
`*Finished patterns for the acts of care*`

From there i moved onto organising the information from the google form i had made. I wrote down what each person did for their acts of care, the emotion they had associated with it, and how long the act had taken. Having everything laid out on a single paper made it easier to compare the responses to the patterns i had to draw with the different colours associated with them

Next I began to assign each of the emotions a specific colour to represent it. I had chosen colours that I thought would stand out clearly against the green of the leaves, making sure each of the emotions felt visually distinct. This step had helped me to think more deeply about how the colours would communicate feelings

![Names and emotions](../assets/week-10/names%20and%20emotions.jpg)
`*all the emotion and the colour that represents them*`

Lastly I began drawing the patterns onto each of the leaves matching the shapes and colours to the emotions and the acts of care the represented. This part of the process had felt most meditative and slowly building the artwork leaf by leaf watching the project start to come together.

![Leaf patterns](../assets/week-10/some%20of%20all%20the%20patterned%20leaves.jpg)
`*Started drawing the patterns onto the leaves*`
