## Feedback Reflection

![Feedback on the drawing ](../assets/week-07/feedback%20on%20drawing.jpg)
`*Feedback that i got about my drawing*`

After coming back to my sketch and reading through the feedback I was given, a few themes that had stood out. I was surprised by how many people had described the sketch as nice, cute and pleasant which had reassured me that the emotional tone of the acts of care was coming through visually. Several of the feedback had aligned with my intentions like colour as a way to present data and the idea that the patterns could express different feelings which had confirmed that using different colours and patterns to encode the emotional nuance was working.

However some of the feedback has revealed gaps I need to address. As few people had said the purpose wasn't clear or that they were unsure on what the drawing represented which told me I needed to strengthen the connection between the visual form and the data. Another comment had questioned whether the garden might be too crowded which is something i need to plan for my layout and the scaling,


## Revised Sketch

Using those insights i revised my sketch by:
1. clarifying the plant to the data mapping (height = time spent, colour = emotional tone, leaf patterns = type of care)

2. spacing the plants more intentionally to avoid visual overcrowding

3. making a small legend to make the purpose more clearer

## Making Sprint

Using rapid prototyping we were to spend 45 minutes producing a hands on experiment with my dataset and visualisation approach using paper. Guided by my skills roadmap I focused on my third priority which was paper craft techniques for 3d forms, I explored on layering, cutting and shaping paper. Since I was working with some standard A4 paper I explored how far I could push the structural possibilities through layering, cutting, shaping and glueing. I had experimented with creating stems, leaves and the plant pot.

This is the outcome of the rapid prototyping

![Making Sprint outcome](../assets/week-07/making%20sprint%201.jpg)
`*making sprint outcome*`

During this session I realised that for the final build I don't want the stems to be made from rolled paper as they are too thick, limit height variation and lack stability. Instead I want to use wire as the stem just as they are or wrapped in paper. And while for the prototype I used just basic a4 paper but for the final version I want to work with thicker and higher quality paper.


## "What if" Variations

We were to share the outcome of our making sprint in pairs where my partner would then propose three what if variations, alternative and provocative directions my project could take but instead we ended up doing it in threes. The what if i got were what i make people write compliments, what if i make it possible so that people could add there acts of care like by making hole in the leaves so that people can hand their acts

For the what if the suggestion what if i made it possible for people to add their own acts of care for example by making holes in the leaves so they could hag their own acts of care i really like the idea of inviting people to take part and contribute their own acts but i was concerned that physically hanging thing on the plant would overcrowd the structure and make it unstable.

But building off that, what if I came up with a version that still allows people to add their own acts of care but in a way that would feel a lot more integrated and symbolic. So instead of hanging things from the leaves people could write their acts of care onto a small paper in the shape of a raindrop and insert it through an opening at the top of the plant pot as if they were watering the plant and helping to take care of it. This keeps the interaction meaningful while also maintaining the clarity and the structure of the visualisation of the plant.

From there i made a new variation drawing of my project that incorporates the what if idea of where people write their own acts of care on paper and insert it into the plant pot. How this differs from my current approach is that it gets people involved with the project, make the project more personal as they add their own act of care into the project.

##Independent Study##

## Project Development & Skill Building

Continuing from the making sprint and the what if variations, I had developed my project further by focusing on the technical skills gaps that were identified from my week 6 roadmap, specifically the need to strengthen the paper craft techniques.

During the 45 minutes rapid prototyping session I had worked with standard a4 paper to explore how layering, cutting, shaping and gluing could form some of the basic components of my visualisation system. I had made a small test of the leaves and the plant pot. This had helped me to understand the limitations of a4 paper and how far I could push it within the short timeframe I had.

As I reflected on the prototype I had realised that the rolled up paper stems were too inconsistent for the height variation that I want to represent in my dataset and building onto this insight I decided that for the final outcome I will use wire for the stems instead allowing for a stronger structure and more control.

The what if activity had also shifted my direction as someone asked "what if people could add their own acts of care by hanging them on the leaves" which i loved the aspect of but i felt that by hanging items it would overcrowd the plant instead i had developed a variation where people can write their own acts of care onto small raindrops and insert them through an opening in the pot which symbolically watering the plant keeps the interaction meaningful while also preserving the structure of the outcome.

Through the process I had learned how the material constrained the shape design decisions, how the construction techniques had influenced the data clarity and how even the small prototypes can reveal what needs to be refined. This iteration moved my project more forward by clarifying the structural system I want to use, strengthening my 3d paper making skills and integrating a more thoughtful and participatory element into the final design.
